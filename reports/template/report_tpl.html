Fairness audit

Run on the German credit data (1,000 real loans), which records sex, age and foreign-worker status. These fields are never model inputs; they are used only to check outcomes against the four-fifths rule.

{{FAIR}}
AttributeGroupTest loansApproval rateImpact ratioFour-fifths rule

Applicants under 25 are approved at 0.68 times the rate of older applicants and are flagged for review. The test set holds only 200 loans, so this demonstrates the check rather than proving a finding; HMDA mortgage data is the planned source for a full audit.

What ships

  • A demo app to score one applicant, batch-score a CSV with reason codes, compare models and review fairness and drift.
  • A full scorecard points table (600 points at 50:1 odds, 20 points to double the odds) that a credit officer can read line by line.
  • Model-risk evidence: AUC, Gini, KS, calibration by decile, PSI, IV strength labels and the fairness audit.
  • A data adapter pattern: Home Credit, Freddie Mac, HMDA or a bank's own extract plug in with one loader.

Known limits

  • This Lending Club extract has no application date, so the test split is random rather than out-of-time. Freddie Mac data would allow a true out-of-time test.
  • Scorecard bins are quantile-based; production bins would also be forced to rise or fall steadily and be signed off by a credit officer.
  • Profit depends on the stated margin and loss assumptions, and reason wording needs compliance review before customer use.
